Why property enquiries go cold in the first hour

Sociam4 minute read

A buyer who fills in a form at 9:40 at night is rarely filling in only yours. They are comparing several projects or partners in the same evening. The first useful reply usually sets the direction of the conversation.

Speed, with substance

Replying fast is necessary, and not enough. "Thank you, our executive will call you" answers nothing. A reply with the floor plan they asked about, the price band and two possible site-visit times moves the decision forward.

The quiet leak

Most lost enquiries are not lost to a rival. They fade because nobody followed up on day three, or because the follow-up repeated day one. A short sequence, written well and sent on the channel the buyer chose, recovers more of these than another round of ads.

Know who to call first

When thirty enquiries arrive in a weekend, the team calls in the order they were received. Asking two or three polite questions up front (budget band, timeline, configuration) lets you call the most serious buyers first, without being rude to anyone.

Where to start

Look at your last fifty enquiries. Note how long the first reply took, what it said and how many received a second follow-up. That one exercise usually shows where the biggest improvement is hiding.
